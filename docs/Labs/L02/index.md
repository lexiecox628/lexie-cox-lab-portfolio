##Preprocessor

I chose the build orientation of my design on the feet of the penguin because it was a good base for the print to build off of. It also had a flat bottom which was helpful because the entire design had something to base it off of. If it was oriented off the top of the penguin it would have to have supports since it is bowl shaped. As a group we put all of our designs in the middle of the plate and close together. This is because we wanted the print to be done as fast as possible due to the time constraint. With putting all the prints in the middle of the plate the hot end assembly does not have to move around as much and they are all oriented in the middle so its not having to move across the plate. 

<img width="1206" height="758" alt="IMG_3884" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d786dcf9-5d36-4003-81bc-1f425c4a954d" />

I did not need to use any kind of supports on my print. If I printed it a different way it would need supports. 

My print did need to be scaled, I scaled it to a total of 134.74%. This is because I felt the original design would be way to small and it would loose its key details. I used the scale tool in order to distribute the design evenly. I feel that if I put in manual numbers it would make the print make more since and I could tweak the numbers a little better than just using the scale tool.

##Lessons Learned

I learned the different types of filament specifically between PETG and PLA. PETG is more flexible and it is tougher. While PLA is easier to print and it melts at a lower temperature. This will be helpful in the future when I am assessing which type of filament I will use for future projects. 

Another thing I learned was why additive manufacturing is useful and the future of design. With additive manufacturing you build something by building pieces by adding layer by layer. Unlike how most things are built today where parts come from cutting the parts out of one bug material. It is also easier to modify projects and find ways to maximize the material without having to waste any of it. 

**Independent Research**

One specific guideline for 3D modeling is to think about the wall thickness and have at least a 1.2 factor of safety because you don't want the wall to collapse or the object to fracture. This is important because when you are printing something you don't want it to collapse or be too brittle. It is also something that allows you to be sure of your work, not only in 3D design but in other projects.

Source: https://bigrep.com/posts/design-for-additive-manufacturing/ 

One FDM specific consideration is bridging. Bridging is when there are two supports or points of contact that are not connected initially but needs to eventually. Since there is nothing between the two points there is nothing to print on. This means you need to add supports or reduce the distance of the points. This is so there is no sagging and the machine actually has something to build off of. 

Source: https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-fdm-3d-printing/
